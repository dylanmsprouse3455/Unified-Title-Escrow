Process first. Documents second.

Work one destination at a time: lender return, financials, recording, then our internal file. Preserve originals and do not create extra copies just because a document looks important.

Main rule: if you pull an original from a package, mark where it came from, make the required copy, and return the original to the same spot.

How Do I Know What to Make Copies Of?

Do not copy every important-looking document. Copy a document when the same information is needed in more than one destination, or when the lender/office procedure specifically requires a copy.

COPY TEST: Ask: “Does this document need to exist in two different places?”
If yes → make a copy. If no → leave it alone unless the lender or office procedure specifically requires one.

Make a copy when…

The lender requires it but the original belongs elsewhere; an original must be recorded but a lender/file copy is also needed; a check or invoice is leaving the office but proof must stay in our file; or office/lender instructions specifically say to retain/send a copy.

Do not automatically copy when…

The document only has one destination and no instruction says to duplicate it. “Important” does not automatically mean “copy.”

Physical workflow: Pull original → mark exact location → make required copy → identify/stamp the copy if office procedure requires it → put original back → send/file the copy where it belongs.
Confirmed training examples: Deed of Trust — copy may go to lender while original goes for recording. Checks — retain copies because originals leave the office. Supporting invoices/statements — retain support when the payment/document leaves. Disbursement Summary/Ledger — provide a lender copy when required while keeping it with the financial records.
